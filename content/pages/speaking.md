I speak about bionic vision, accessible science, and what it means to build the technology I may one day use myself. I've been blind since age five, and I research cortical visual prostheses at UC Santa Barbara's Bionic Vision Lab — which makes me both the engineer and the end-user of my field. I'm available for keynotes, conference talks, panels, podcasts, university seminars, and corporate accessibility events, in **English, Portuguese, or Spanish**, in person or remote.

**To book me:** email [lucas@braillemind.com](mailto:lucas@braillemind.com?subject=Speaking%20inquiry) with the event, date, format, and audience. I usually reply within two business days.

## Topics I speak about

- **The engineer and the patient are the same person.** What bionic vision can and can't do today, why restoring sight is harder than headlines suggest, and what it's like to build implants for a population you belong to.
- **Designing with us, not for us.** Lessons from our research on what implantees actually want from visual prostheses — and why assistive technology fails when disabled users are left out of the room. Based on my co-first-authored study in *Translational Vision Science & Technology*.
- **Doing science without sight.** How I read papers, write code, analyze data, and run experiments as a blind neuroscientist — the tools, the workarounds, and what labs gain when they include disabled scientists.
- **From Brazil to the Bionic Vision Lab.** My path from a blind kid in São Paulo with few accessible resources to the first blind student in Carnegie Mellon's Biomedical Engineering department and a PhD in visual prosthetics.
