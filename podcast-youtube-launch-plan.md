# Braille Mind — Podcast & YouTube Launch Plan

*For Lucas Gil Nadolskis. Audio-first, fully accessible workflow, no editor required until the show earns one. Everything here is buildable with what you own today: a Blue Yeti, a Lumina camera, JAWS, and a quiet room.*

---

## 1. The show

**Name:** Braille Mind (matches the site, the newsletter, and the future @braillemind accounts — one brand everywhere).

**One-line pitch:** A blind neuroscientist building bionic vision talks about what technology can and can't restore — with the scientists who build it and the people who live with it.

**Format:** 25–35 minutes. Two alternating episode types:

- **Solo essays** — your blog posts and LinkedIn long-posts, spoken and expanded. Zero scheduling, zero guests, recordable any evening. These are your volume engine.
- **Conversations** — one guest, 40 min recorded, trimmed to ~30. These are your reach engine, because every guest shares the episode with their audience.

**Cadence:** Every other week to start (26 episodes/year). Weekly kills unedited shows; monthly kills momentum. Batch-record so you always have 3 episodes banked before anything publishes.

**Language:** English main feed. If it takes off, a monthly Portuguese episode ("Braille Mind em Português") is an uncontested lane — there is no Portuguese-language podcast at this intersection.

## 2. Episode Zero — script skeleton (10–12 minutes, record this first)

The trailer/origin episode. Purpose: anyone who finds the show later starts here. It's your LinkedIn reintroduction post, expanded to audio. Structure with rough timing:

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:45).** No music, no intro, just: "When I tell people what I do for a living, there's always a pause. I'm a neuroscientist. I work on restoring vision. And I've been blind since I was a child. This show lives inside that pause."
2. **Who I am (0:45–3:30).** São Paulo → losing sight at five → books that arrived months late → University of Minnesota → first blind student in CMU's Biomedical Engineering department → UCSB Bionic Vision Lab. Keep the register you set on LinkedIn: professional, no drama, the facts carry the weight.
3. **What the show is (3:30–6:30).** The two questions everything comes back to: what can technology actually restore, and who gets to define "useful"? Preview the lanes: how bionic vision really works, how I do science with a screen reader, conversations with builders and implantees, and the accessibility fights nobody sees.
4. **Why me, stated plainly (6:30–8:30).** The engineer-and-end-user position. Two years on Neuralink's BlindSight Consumer Advisory Board. To the lab's knowledge, the only blind researcher working in sight restoration. Say it once, cleanly, and move on.
5. **The ask (8:30–10:00).** Follow the show, subscribe at braillemind.com, and one specific request: "If you know someone living with a visual prosthesis, or building one, I want to talk to them."

Record it three times end-to-end; keep the best take whole. Do not sentence-surgery it in the editor — momentum reads better than polish.

## 3. First ten episodes (after Episode Zero)

Alternating solo (S) and conversation (C). Solo topics are your existing written material — already drafted, already in your voice:

1. **(S) The bionic eye doesn't send pictures.** Phosphenes, why electrode count isn't the story, the smart bionic eye idea. (From LinkedIn Post 4 / the NLM essay.)
2. **(C) Michael Beyeler.** Your PI. What the field gets wrong about "restoring vision," the deep-learning stimulation work that just made news. Easiest possible first guest; his audience is your audience.
3. **(S) How I read papers.** The ar5iv workflow, PDF hell, what publishers owe blind scientists. (Post 3.)
4. **(C) An implantee.** Argus II or Orion recipient from your TVST study network. The episode only you can make — researchers can't get this conversation, and implantees don't have a platform. This episode is your calling card.
5. **(S) What implantees actually want.** The TVST paper, acuity vs. independence, "design with us, not for us."
6. **(C) Frank Elavsky.** Accessible data visualization, Data Navigator, Skeleton, whether charts must be visual at all.
7. **(S) First blind student in the department.** The CMU story. Tooling is fixable. What PIs should know. (Post 5.)
8. **(C) Jonathan Mosen or Chancey Fleet.** The blind-community side: what neurotech looks like from the assistive-tech world, healthy skepticism about cure narratives — an important counterweight episode that earns trust with blind listeners.
9. **(S) Two years advising Neuralink.** The meta-lessons you can share: "technically impressive" vs. "worth surgery," the Tuesday-morning test. (Post 6.)
10. **(C) A vision scientist outside your lab** (Nicole Rust, Brad Wyble, or an ARVO/VSS contact): what the brain does that no implant replicates soon — prediction, filling-in, attention.

## 4. The zero-editor production pipeline

**Recording — solo:** REAPER + OSARA. OSARA (osara.reaperaccessibility.com) is the free extension that makes REAPER the standard DAW for blind audio engineers; the REAPER license is $60 after a fully-functional 60-day trial. Keyboard-driven end to end under JAWS. Learn exactly five operations and ignore the rest: record, play, split at cursor (S), delete selected item, render to WAV. That is a complete podcast edit.

**Recording — interviews:** Zoom, with "record separate audio files for each participant" enabled in settings. Zoom is solidly JAWS-accessible and every guest already has it. (Riverside/Zencastr have better fidelity but shakier screen-reader support — revisit later, not a launch blocker.)

**Blue Yeti settings:** cardioid pattern (the dial position matters — one click from the headphone volume side on most models; have someone confirm once), gain at ~30%, mouth a hand's width from the side of the mic (the Yeti is side-address — speak into the flat side with the logo, not the top), pop filter if you have one. Record in the most furniture-and-fabric-filled room available; a closet full of clothes beats a home office.

**Post-production — Auphonic (auphonic.com).** This replaces your editor. Create one preset: adaptive leveler ON, loudness target −16 LUFS (podcast standard), noise & hum reduction ON, filtering ON, your intro/outro files attached, output MP3 96kbps mono + WAV. Every episode after setup: upload raw file, pick preset, press start, download the finished episode. The web interface is screen-reader workable; 2 hours/month free covers your cadence at launch ($11/mo if you exceed it).

**Transcripts:** Auphonic can generate them (Whisper built in), or run Whisper locally. Publish the transcript on braillemind.com with every episode — for you this is a brand statement, and it's also YouTube SEO for free.

**Music:** one 10-second intro sting, licensed once (Premium Beat ~$50, or a musician friend). Don't scroll libraries for weeks; pick in one sitting.

## 5. Publishing

**Podcast host:** RSS.com or Transistor ($19/mo, cleaner accessibility record) — either gives you one upload that syndicates to Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and everywhere else via RSS. Set the podcast website field to braillemind.com. Add a Podcast section to the site nav (the build script can take a podcast.md page — same pattern as speaking.md).

**YouTube channel:** youtube.com/@braillemind (grab the handle when you create it — same claim-the-name logic as Instagram). Two upload types at launch:
- Full episodes: the Lumina single-take video for conversations recorded on Zoom (record the Zoom video too) or a branded static-card video for solo episodes — Auphonic can generate an audiogram video (waveform over your branded card) automatically, so solo episodes cost you nothing extra.
- Later, with the editor hire from the visibility plan: Shorts cut from each episode (3–5 per episode is standard).

**The fixed video setup (one-time, with a sighted friend for 15 minutes):** Lumina mounted on a small tripod or monitor mount, never handheld. Chair position marked on the floor (tape). One key light — a basic LED panel at 45° from your face, brightness set once, tape over the controls. Friend confirms framing with Lumina's auto-framing ON, then nothing moves again. After this, every recording is correctly framed by default and you never need to check.

**Thumbnails:** three template PNGs are in the repo (`youtube-thumbnails/` — delivered alongside this plan): solo-episode, guest-episode (space for a guest photo on the right), and topic/explainer. All 1280×720 in the Braille Mind dark style. Making a thumbnail = adding the episode title text; anyone can do it in two minutes, or I can generate them per-episode when you tell me the titles.

## 6. Launch sequence

1. Install REAPER + OSARA; do one 5-minute throwaway recording to learn the five operations.
2. Record Episode Zero (three full takes, keep the best).
3. Record solo episodes 1 and 3 (scripts already exist as posts).
4. Auphonic preset + one music sting. Process all three.
5. Set up the podcast host, submit to Apple/Spotify (takes a few days to appear — do this a week before launch).
6. Create @braillemind YouTube channel; upload the three episodes as audiogram videos.
7. Launch day: Episode Zero + Episode 1 together (shows always launch with 2+ episodes so new listeners can binge), announcement on LinkedIn (that's Post 8's slot — adapt it), X, Bluesky, and the newsletter's first real send.
8. Invite Beyeler for Episode 2 the same week.

**Success metric for the first six months:** shipped every two weeks without missing. Not downloads. Consistency is the entire game at the start; the audience compounds later.

## 7. What NOT to do (the no-editor guardrails)

- No multi-track music beds, no mid-roll sound design, no chapter art per episode. That's editor work; you don't have an editor.
- No sentence-level surgery in REAPER. Cut only: dead air at the ends, coughing fits, catastrophic tangents. A human "um" is fine — your medium is credibility, not NPR polish.
- No daily or weekly cadence promises in public. Say "new episodes every other week" only after three are banked.
- No buying the specialized camera yet. The Lumina + fixed setup is above the bar for podcast video. Revisit cameras when the editor exists and Shorts become the growth engine.
